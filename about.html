Professional Bio

I am a Lecturer in Mathematics and Statistics at Walailak University, Thailand, and an applied mathematician and data scientist. My research connects mathematical modelling, statistical and machine learning methods, and interpretable computational approaches to study complex biological, ecological and public-health systems.

My work spans mathematical epidemiology, population and ecological dynamics, climate-related risks, forecasting, and Formal Concept Analysis (FCA). I am particularly interested in combining mechanistic models—such as ordinary differential equations and discrete-time systems—with data-driven methods to understand system behaviour, evaluate uncertainty and support scientifically defensible decisions.

My ecological research has examined climate change, fisheries and stage-structured population dynamics, while my applied data-science work includes interpretable ensemble learning and climate-informed disease-risk analysis. I have teaching experience at undergraduate and graduate levels, including co-instruction of graduate courses at the University of Toronto.

Looking ahead, I aim to develop reusable mathematical and explainable-AI frameworks that remain reliable under changing conditions and can support practical decisions in public health, environmental management and other data-rich settings, with particular interest in applications relevant to Thailand.

Core Research Themes

  • Mathematical Epidemiology and Disease Dynamics — transmission models, outbreak processes, epidemic forecasting and intervention evaluation.
  • Mathematical Ecology and Climate Change — population dynamics, stage-structured models, fisheries, environmental stress and resilience.
  • Data Science, Machine Learning and Forecasting — statistical learning, time-series analysis and scientific prediction from real-world datasets.
  • Explainable AI and Formal Concept Analysis — concept lattices, interpretable machine learning and hybrid mechanistic–data-driven frameworks.
  • Optimization, Model Reliability and Decision Support — an emerging direction exploring uncertainty, model robustness, network methods and resource-allocation problems.

Research goal: Develop mathematically sound, transparent and reproducible methods that connect scientific understanding to decisions with practical value. Explore the research themes →

Emerging Research Directions

My developing research agenda explores how to recognize when predictive models become unreliable as biological or environmental conditions change; how to integrate mechanistic modelling and explainable AI; and how mathematical optimization might improve responses to climate-related health and ecological risks. These are areas for investigation and collaboration, not claims of completed results.

Student Research and Collaboration

I welcome discussions with students interested in learning how mathematics and computational tools can address research questions using genuine data. Potential introductory areas include:

  • Analysing climate, environmental or public-health time series in Python or R.
  • Exploring discrete-time population or compartmental disease models.
  • Comparing forecasting approaches and evaluating model errors.
  • Visualizing and interpreting patterns using Formal Concept Analysis.
  • Studying simple resource-allocation or network-optimization problems.

Projects can be scoped to students' mathematical and programming backgrounds, with attention to clear research questions, sound methods and reproducible analysis.
