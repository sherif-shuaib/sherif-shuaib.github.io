About the Lab

MAIDES Lab

The MAIDES Lab — Mathematics, AI, Disease, Ecology, and Systems — brings together research interests in mathematical modelling, epidemiology, ecological dynamics, data analytics, and explainable machine learning. Our work builds on existing mathematical and computational research while exploring important questions in biological and environmental systems.

Our guiding question is: How can we combine mathematical mechanisms, real-world data, and interpretable computational methods to understand changing systems and support more reliable decisions? We value reproducibility, careful validation, transparent assumptions, and meaningful extensions of prior research.

A typical project moves from an established research question and a verified gap to suitable data, mathematical or statistical modelling, careful validation, and evidence-based interpretation. Optimization or decision-support tools are considered when the research and available data justify them.

Core research themes

Five Connected Research Themes

Mathematical Epidemiology & Disease Dynamics

Mechanistic ODE and discrete-time models of disease transmission, thresholds, intervention strategies, and epidemiological uncertainty. Applications include dengue and other infectious diseases.

Mathematical Ecology & Climate Change

Population and stage-structured modelling, fisheries dynamics, ecological persistence, climate impacts, and the response of biological systems to environmental change.

Data Science, AI & Forecasting

Time-series analysis, statistical learning, machine learning, temporal validation, and forecasting using environmental, epidemiological, agricultural, and ecological datasets.

Explainable AI & Formal Concept Analysis

Interpretable predictive models, FCA and concept lattices, explainable ensemble methods, hybrid mechanistic–AI approaches, and investigation of model reliability under changing conditions.

Optimization, Model Reliability & Decision Support

An emerging research direction: mathematical optimization, network resilience, uncertainty-aware decisions, and tools connecting validated predictions with practical actions in health and environmental management.

Looking ahead · 2027–2031

Questions We Want to Investigate

These are prospective research directions, not claims that the gaps have already been established. Each requires a focused review of existing literature, feasibility assessment, and validation before becoming a research project.

01

When should a model stop trusting its forecasts?

Explore whether interpretable structural changes, including FCA-based representations, can help detect changes in prediction reliability and guide model reassessment.

FCA · time series · concept drift · uncertainty
02

How resilient is healthcare access during extreme weather?

Investigate network-based measures of access disruption and possible interventions during flooding or other climate-related disruptions in Southern Thailand.

Graph theory · optimization · spatial data
03

Which heat-protection actions are most effective?

Study uncertainty-aware allocation of limited heat-protection resources while considering feasibility and fairness across communities.

Mathematical optimization · climate data
04

What makes ecological recovery last?

Develop and test stage-structured or discrete-time models of ecological persistence and climate stress, including possible applications to fisheries and habitat restoration.

Population dynamics · ODEs · stochastic models
For undergraduate students

Explore Research With Us

Third-year students interested in applied mathematics, statistics, programming, or real-world datasets are welcome to discuss possible supervised projects. Prior research experience is not required; topics and availability depend on preparation, scope, and supervision capacity.

Mathematical modelling

Simple ODE or difference-equation models, simulations, sensitivity analysis, and interpretation of biological dynamics.

Data analysis & forecasting

Explore public datasets, visualize patterns, compare baseline forecasting models, and assess out-of-sample performance.

Explainable AI & FCA

Study concept lattices, simple interpretable classifiers, and how model explanations relate to data.

Climate & environmental systems

Investigate climate indicators, fisheries, ecological patterns, or local environmental questions using accessible data.

Useful starting skills: introductory calculus, statistics, algebra, or Python. You do not need to know all of these before making contact.
